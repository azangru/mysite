This has been a multi-year exercise in embarrassment and procrastination...

As a web developer, I have long been embarrassed by my website. Primarily this was because for its last iteration (in 2015 or so), I succumbed to the hype and built it with Gatsby. I vaguely remember seeing a discussion of static site generators on Slack, where the participants agreed that Gatsby was "awesome". I remember watching Kyle Mathews' talks about a universal data layer built with graphql. I remember getting hooked...

It took me several years to grow up and recognize that nobody should need graphql or, god forbid, react for a measly personal website. In 2021, I cut a branch in my site's github repo, and called it `migrate-to-eleventy`...

Now, as I am writing these words, it is already 2026. What have I been doing all these years?

Procrastinating, that's what. Incapacitated by analysis paralysis.

Thing is, over the years of posting online on a different platform, I gradually learnt about my writing habits. It's funny — going on a tangent now — to think that during my first attempt to create a personal website, I was specifically looking for a tool that would make it possible to build a multilingual site, which is how I chose Middleman to build the first version. As if I were actually going to write in two languages. Ha! Time showed this was pure fantasy. It also showed that I have very little to say. I don't quite see the point (except for marketing, but marketing is yucky) in adopting an instructor's stance and rehashing stuff that is far better presented in documentation or in conference talks. And I am not a deep enough thinker to produce genuinely useful and original technical content, such as, say, Roman Komarov does at [kizu.dev](https://kizu.dev/).

And yet, when I looked at my statistics on the other platform, I saw that over the years, I have posted over 8000 entries there.

What were they about?

They were mainly short texts, often written in response to something that I saw or heard online. Since the strongest emotions are anger, annoyance, or indignation, most of my scribblings are in a negative tone. They are similar to what people tend to write on Twitter, Bluesky, or Mastodon; only I never had an account there. And besides, I don't know if I would be welcome there, especially not on Mastodon, because I came to recognise that I do not share the ideology that is very prominent there.
