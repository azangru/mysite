Alex Russell [writes](https://toot.cafe/@slightlyoff/116177948324583692):

> Another day, another email documenting a vast waste of resources on a React upgrade.

> You know who doesn't have to do this nonsense? Folks who write to the web platform. Your old code still works. Your new code works even better.

> Why? Because unlike React, we take the platform's role *as a platform* quite seriously.

> If your org is trapped on the treadmill, you should plan One Last Upgrade: get to React 19 (which *finally* has Web Components support) -- or even better, Preact -- then *get the hell off*. Move your componentry to WC incrementally, bit by bit, until the React part of your codebase is nothing more than `<web-component-app></web-component-app>` 

I am sympathetic with this sentiment; but damn, Alex is an insufferable tease! Has he personally worked through de-reacting a large app? Maybe he has (after all, he has an influential position at Microsoft, and advises everyone within his reach to ditch React); but if he has, why not share the experience? For example, web platform still doesn't have a scoped custom elements registry — would this be a problem for large codebases? Is there a danger of naming collisions? Also, with React, you have well-trodden paths for doing things: a router, a dependency injection mechanism, a data fetching library, various global state management options. With web components, you are on your own. What patterns for structuring large applications have proven to be resilient? What do we migrate our reduxes, zustands, or react-queries to?

Where do we learn proper web engineering?
